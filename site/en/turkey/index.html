EV charging stations in Turkey

Turkey has 13,380 public charging stations, run by 187 operators and spread across all 81 provinces. 7,834 of them are fast DC, 5,546 are standard AC, and 3,654 deliver 150 kW or more. Driving an EV from Georgia into Turkey is now a question of planning, not of risk.

What changes the moment you cross the border

Turkey charges to the European standard. Fast charging runs on CCS2, slow charging on Type 2, and there is effectively nothing else: CHAdeMO is on just 188 stations, and GB/T on none at all.

That single sentence matters most to drivers coming from Georgia. GB/T is ordinary in Georgia because a large share of imported cars are Chinese and arrive with that inlet. In Turkey such a car cannot fast charge at all. If your car also has CCS2 you are fine; if it only has GB/T, all that is left is slow Type 2 charging, and only where the car has a Type 2 inlet. Check which inlet you actually have before planning the drive.

What charging costs in Turkey

Turkish operators publish national tariffs, so the same network costs the same in İstanbul and in Trabzon. The median fast DC price is 14.74 ₺ per kWh, ranging from 8.99 ₺ to 15.90 ₺. On AC the median is 9.99 ₺, ranging from 8.49 ₺ to 11.99 ₺. 69 percent of stations publish a tariff.

A practical yardstick: charging a 60 kWh battery from 20 to 80 percent means 36 kWh, which at the median tariff is about 531 lira. Prices are in lira and the exchange rate moves, so convert on the day you travel.

The road from the Georgian border

Between the Sarp border gate and Trabzon there are 68 fast chargers within 5 km of the coastal road, 28 of them rated 150 kW or more. The longest stretch between two neighbouring chargers is about 40 km.

Along the whole road from Sarp to İstanbul, following the Black Sea coast, there are 842 fast chargers within 5 km of the route, 348 of them at 150 kW or more. That drive, with its stops and its cost, has its own guide.

Live status and the app

The Turkish registry does not publish whether a charger is busy right now, so for Turkish stations the app shows the connector count and the tariff rather than free bays. In Georgia live status works and refreshes in real time.

In the app, Turkey is switched on in Settings, in the country list. Route planning needs no switch at all: if the route crosses Turkey, the Turkish chargers are downloaded automatically and counted into the stops.

Frequently asked questions

How many EV chargers are there in Turkey?

13,380 public stations run by 187 operators. 7,834 of them are fast DC and 3,654 deliver 150 kW or more. The figures come from the official EPDK registry.

Which connector do I need in Turkey?

CCS2 for fast charging, Type 2 for slow. CCS2 is on 7,832 stations and Type 2 on 7,344. CHAdeMO appears on only 188, and GB/T does not exist in Turkey at all, so a Chinese car with a GB/T-only inlet cannot fast charge there.

What does charging cost in Turkey?

The median fast DC tariff is 14.74 ₺ per kWh and the median AC tariff 9.99 ₺. 69 percent of stations publish a price, and most operators run one national tariff.

Which province has the most chargers?

İstanbul with 2,827 stations, followed by Ankara with 1,359.

Does Turkey show live charger availability?

No. The Turkish registry publishes no real-time status, so GeoCharge shows the connector count and the tariff for Turkish stations instead of free bays. Live status works in Georgia.

How far is the first fast charger from the Georgian border?

On the stretch from the Sarp border gate to Trabzon there are 68 fast chargers within 5 km of the road, and the longest stretch between any two of them is about 40 km.

How it is calculated

The formula is one step: battery capacity times the share you are adding, then the kilowatt hours times the tariff. The only complication is that fast chargers slow down sharply above 80 percent, so the time estimate is optimistic beyond that point.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate charging cost?

Multiply battery capacity by the share you are adding, then multiply the kilowatt hours by the tariff. A 60 kWh battery from 20 to 80 percent is 36 kWh, which at a 0.78 GEL DC tariff comes to 28.08 GEL.

What is the current tariff in Georgia?

The median fast DC tariff is 0.78 GEL per kWh and the median slow AC tariff is 0.65. The full picture by network is on the tariffs page.

Why does the real receipt differ from the calculation?

Two reasons. The energy that reaches the battery is always slightly less than the charger draws, since some is lost as heat. And some operators bill separately for occupying the bay after charging ends.
